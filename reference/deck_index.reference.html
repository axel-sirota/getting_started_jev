Why platforms, why now

  • 90% of orgs run an internal platform, 76% have a dedicated team (DORA 2025)
  • DevOps gave teams autonomy, and with it cost, risk and duplication
  • Why now: AI amplifies platform quality, good or bad (DORA 2025)
  • In banks the real driver is governance, not speed
Source: Google Cloud: 2025 DORA Report·CNCF Platforms White Paper·DX: 2024 DORA highlights
nearly everyone already has some kind of platform. Today's question is whether yours removes work or just moves it. And DORA 2025 found AI only pays off when the platform underneath is good.

Anatomy of a golden path

  • Opinionated and supported (Spotify's definition)
  • Covers the common 80%, not everything
  • Defaults you can override
  • Fails with a clear error and the fix
Source: Spotify Engineering: Golden Paths·State of Platform Engineering Vol 4 (PDF)·platformengineering.org: Golden cage syndrome
Spotify built golden paths to kill "rumour-driven development". Opinionated, supported, and you can still step off it as long as you own what you change.

From project to product

  • A platform is an internal product, developers are its customers
  • One named product owner, not a committee
  • Research real developer journeys before building
  • Ship thin, measure, reprioritise
Source: DORA capability page·Martin Fowler / Evan Bottcher
a project ends at launch, a product starts there. Researching real journeys is the step most platform teams skip.

How the platform team works with everyone else

  • Collaborate while a capability is still being discovered
  • Move to X-as-a-Service once it's stable
  • Facilitate when a team needs uplift
  • Start with a Thinnest Viable Platform, even a wiki page
Source: Team Topologies: TVP·Team Topologies: TVP wiki template·Umbrex: Team Topologies summary
the Thinnest Viable Platform can literally be a wiki page listing what you offer and how to get help. Start there, not with a portal.

Buyer vs user

  • Buyers (CIO, risk, compliance) fund it and set the controls
  • Users (engineers) live with it every day
  • The owner balances both and needs authority to say no
  • Users are captive, so build proxies for choice
Source: Practitioner thread: rebranding platform teams?·Practitioner thread: customers can't leave
engineers feel this tension every time a new control lands on them. The platform owner's job is to absorb it, not pass it on.

The platform API is the product

  • Declarative, versioned, stored in Git
  • Portal, CLI, IDE and AI assistants are skins on top
  • Swap the implementation without breaking the contract
  • Banks already do this (Millennium bcp on Crossplane)
Source: Crossplane adopters·Syntasso: platform APIs

Golden path vs golden cage

  • Earned vs mandatory
  • Override when needed vs no escape hatch
  • Clear error with the fix vs cryptic wrapper error
  • Teams contribute vs the central team approves everything
Source: platformengineering.org: Golden cage syndrome·DORA capability page
the golden cage test is one question. If this were optional tomorrow, would people still use it?

Debrief: mandate the outcome, pave the path
